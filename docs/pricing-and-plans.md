**Purpose:** Let merchants discover they have a problem. The first scan is the marketing moment — generous on surface, tight on actionability. Showing the worst finding with full detail (file, line, snippet) creates maximum urgency while keeping the rest locked behind upgrade.

Optional permissions on Free (gc-4n0y): the Settings Permissions card and the scan page's "checks skipped because permissions weren't granted" notice show on every plan, and Home's welcome card has an optional, non-blocking line ("Optional: allow product, page, redirect, and translation checks for a deeper first scan.") that opens the same App Bridge scopes request. The scan worker never plan-gated these audits, so a Free shop that grants the scopes gets those checks. Broken links stays Standard+ by plan, not by permission: the Settings card tells a Free shop so, and the coverage notice drops that category for Free. Plan-withheld types (`app/lib/plan-finding-types.ts`: Broken links and checkout sunset when the plan lacks them) are never shown in full on Free, even on a downgraded shop's older scans: they are excluded from the preview and so from "Start here", but still counted as locked in the teaser and lanes. Home's lane "Start here" chip follows the block's first finding when the block shows. More checks only means more findings to tease: Free still sees up to 5 in full (never more than half), and the teaser counts the rest.

Below the preview, the scan page's upgrade teaser counts the locked findings by consequence lane (for example "12 more findings on Standard: Found by Google & AI (5), Speed (4), Housekeeping (3).") and its CTA is a plain top-level link to the Managed Pricing plan page (same as the Settings upgrade buttons); the click is recorded by a best-effort keepalive POST to `/app/upgrade` that never blocks navigation. Known-malicious findings are never counted as locked: they are shown in full on every plan. The teaser's shown / clicked / converted events are recorded once per merchant (gc-97k.4). `converted` counts a Free -> paid upgrade by any merchant who was shown the teaser, not only one whose click was recorded, because the keepalive click ping can be lost when the navigation unloads the iframe. The teaser and the Settings plan tiles share one CTA copy source (`app/lib/trial-cta.ts`, gc-97k.8): a Free shop with no `Shop.everPaidAt` and no `BillingEvent` sees "Start 7-day free trial" (teaser body: "Try Standard free for 7 days to see every file, line, and fix."); a shop that has ever had a paid plan (`everPaidAt` set, any `BillingEvent`, or on a paid plan now) sees "Upgrade to Standard" / "Upgrade to Professional", because Shopify grants the Managed Pricing trial only once per shop. `everPaidAt` is stamped once by every reconcile that observes a paid plan (the `plan_handle` redirect and the stale on-load backstop alike), so a paid period seen only by a backstop reconcile (which records no `BillingEvent`) still counts; it was backfilled from `BillingEvent` history and the current plan. Accepted gap: shop/redact deletes the Shop row and its `BillingEvent`s, so a reinstall after redact looks never-paid.

"Start here" block (gc-bn0x, all plans): the scan page (unfiltered view) and Home's latest-scan view open with the scan's 3 most important findings, one card per finding (gc-g034: cards across, stacked on a phone), each in its primary lane's colours with the lane chip (malicious code and an affected-app advisory are a red "Security risk" card), with "from {App}" when known, one line on what it costs, where it lives, and a "See how to fix" button to its row. One shared severity is said once in the intro; mixed severities get a badge per card. One ranking everywhere (`app/lib/top-findings.ts`): malicious first, then severity, then consequence urgency, then lane order, then oldest first; a repeated finding type gives way to another type of the same severity. Ignored findings are excluded. Standard and Professional rank the whole scan. Free ranks ONLY what Free already shows in full (its preview rows plus malicious findings), so the block is always a subset of the visible preview and never reveals a locked finding; the preview picker uses the same ranking inside its one-per-lane rule so the two agree.

Affected-app advisory (`APP_INCIDENT_ADVISORY`, gc-14x5, all plans, never paywalled, in-app only): when the PUBLISHED theme loads the script of an app named in a public security incident report (curated list with a citable source and a review date: `app/data/app-incident-advisories.server.ts`), or has that app's theme app embed turned on in `config/settings_data.json` (gc-14x5.1), the scan stores ONE finding per listed app. "Loads" is literal: a `<script src>` tag or a `script_tag` filter on the listed exact host, in a Liquid file the storefront renders, outside comments and Liquid that never runs. The embed signal is a listed `shopify://apps/<app>/blocks/<block>` type that is not `"disabled": true`; a disabled embed never fires. The host signal wins when both match, and the stored description and card say which one matched ("Loads code from <App> (<host>)" vs "Has the <App> app embed turned on"). Entries as of 2026-10-26: Hulk Product Options (host and embed), Swym Wishlist Plus (embed), Selleasy (host and embed), 17TRACK (embed; third-party report lists it as unconfirmed, added on the owner's call, gc-dr7a). It states two facts (the theme loads the app's code or has its embed on, and the public report exists) and never that the store was affected. On EVERY plan it is shown as a security risk (gc-q5z8): a red "Security risk" banner per advisory (the shared `SecurityRiskBanner`, also used for known malicious code, which comes first) at the top of Home and of the scan page, naming the app, quoting the entry's own incident wording and date (`report`), saying what GhostCode found (embed turned on, or the script host and file), linking the vendor statement or report, and noting "shown on every plan". On the scan page the banner holds the "What to check" steps, Copy steps and the Ignore control on every plan, and is never inside the list, its Type filter or a lane filter (`OWN_SECTION_FINDING_TYPES`); "Total findings" notes "N findings: k security risks (above), m leftover items (below).", and names the lines when the k risks are made of more findings ("1 security risk (3 findings, above)") so the leftover count adds up (gc-xtno). On Home its primary button "See what to check" links to that banner. While a scan runs, or after one failed, Home keeps the most recent successful scan's banners under the line "From your last completed scan on <date>, while the new scan runs." (". Your latest scan did not complete." after a failure), so they never vanish during a rescan (gc-xtno). Wherever a removal-safety badge shows, a security finding gets the red "Security risk: check before anything else", never "Likely safe" (gc-xtno). Ignoring is otherwise a paid feature: the scan page action lets a Free shop ignore ONLY an advisory, and never ignore by app. Plan gating is the same as for known malicious code, through ONE shared list (`NEVER_PAYWALLED_FINDING_TYPES` / `isNeverPaywalledFindingType`): it never takes a Free preview slot, never raises the preview count, is never counted as hidden by the teaser or the return banner, and is always eligible for Free's "Start here". Export stays a paid feature for every type. Unlike malicious code it honours ignores (an ignored advisory has no banner and is not a security risk anywhere). A non-ignored advisory turns Home's findings tile critical, labelled "N findings, k security risks" (gc-q5z8); k counts distinct risks, one per banner (one per malicious domain, one per advisory), never finding lines (gc-xtno). It is in no consequence lane, in no theme health category row, and in none of the summary email's counts (new, fixed, still in your theme), so it can never cause or change an email. Home's Theme health has its own "App security advisories" check (gc-q5z8): a failed red chip and the red verdict "An app named in a security incident is in your theme." while one is open (never the green "No known malicious code found."), a green chip when the check ran and nothing is open, "Not checked" when the check is live but the scan predates it, and no chip while the check is dark. Home's top card names it in one summary line ("1 app security advisory, 12 leftover items. See all"), so the tile's total is leftover items plus advisories (plus ignored malicious code). "Start here" ranks it below malicious code and above every other finding. It is soft-launched: live only when `APP_INCIDENT_ADVISORY_LIVE_ENABLED` is exactly `true` AND at least one list entry has not passed its `reviewBy` date. A draft-theme scan, or a scan after the last entry expired, records the type as not live, so nothing is reported new or fixed across those scans. While an advisory is stored for a scan, its host is left out of that scan's "Unrecognized Scripts". Operating it (turning it on, entries, kill switch, the DELETE required before a rollback): `docs/runbooks/app-incident-advisory.md`.

Code in navigation menus (`MENU_INJECTION`, gc-7gjj.1, all plans, never paywalled, in-app only, DARK behind `MENU_INJECTION_LIVE_ENABLED`): with the optional `read_online_store_navigation` permission (the same one the URL-redirect check uses), each scan reads every navigation menu item's label and link through the Admin `menus` query (at most 250 menus and 5,000 items; past that the check is recorded as capped) and flags an item whose label holds a `<script>`, an `on*=` handler inside a tag, a frame/embed/meta refresh, a `javascript:`/`vbscript:`/`data:text/html` link, or a listed malicious domain. Plain markup merchants use on purpose (`<b>`, `<span>`, an icon `<img>`) and entity-encoded text are never flagged. One HIGH finding per item, stored as `menus/{menuId}/items/{itemId}` with the menu path and a bounded excerpt (at most 200 characters). It is shown as a red Security risk banner on the scan page and Home on every plan (never a Free preview slot, never counted as hidden, through `NEVER_PAYWALLED_FINDING_TYPES`), with the steps to check, an "Open menu" admin link and Ignore; unlike malicious code it honours ignores (an `onclick` in a label can be on purpose). Theme health gets a "Menu labels" check (red verdict when something is found). Without the permission the check is skipped (the "checks skipped" banner names "Menu labels") and a skipped or capped check never marks an earlier finding fixed. GhostCode never edits menus. The Settings permission card still says "URL redirects" for this permission (relabel is owner decision D4, not done).

Known malicious code seen outside theme files (gc-0htk, all plans, never paywalled, DARK behind `RUNTIME_MALICIOUS_CHECK_LIVE_ENABLED`): the curated known-malicious list (`app/data/malicious-domains.server.ts`) is also matched against two things the app already reads, with no new outbound request. (1) **The served homepage**, from the one storefront read per scan (so it also needs `SCRIPT_TAG_SUNSET_LIVE_ENABLED`, published theme only): only places that load, run or send something count: a `<script src>`, a `<link href>`, an app's script tag (Shopify's asyncLoad list), an `<iframe src>`, a `<form action>`, a URL in an import map, a URL inside inline JavaScript (gc-3y89) and a URL inside an `on*=` event-handler attribute of any element, after HTML character references are decoded (gc-7gjj.4, labelled "Named in JavaScript attached to an element in the page"; Dawn/Horizon `onload="this.media='all'"` handlers name no host and never count). A `<link>` that is only a connection hint (`preconnect`, `dns-prefetch`) still counts but is labelled "Named in a connection hint in the page", never "Loaded". Page text, `<a href>`, images, JSON data blocks and the bodies of inert elements (`<textarea>`, `<title>`, `<template>`, `<noscript>`) never count, and an HTML or JS comment never counts for any entry (a commented-out tag on the served page does not load). Known limits: a URL inside a JavaScript string counts (merchant text a theme serializes into a script can match); a loader that builds its URL at run time names no host; and once one start tag on the page never closes, later handlers are not read and a tag written inside an attribute value is read as a tag (linear-time bound). A hit is a `MALICIOUS_SCRIPT` finding filed under `storefront/homepage` (one per listed domain, HIGH, line 0; stored text is fixed-shape: the listed domain, how the page used it, and the matched host; no page text, path or query string), left out when the scan's theme files already report the same domain. Same type, so every never-paywalled rule applies unchanged through `NEVER_PAYWALLED_FINDING_TYPES` (never a Free preview slot, never counted as hidden, shown regardless of ignores, in the summary email's counts like any malicious code) and the shared `SecurityRiskBanner`, which says "Your live homepage uses X" and that it is not in the theme files. Exports (PDF, CSV, JSON) name its place "Your live homepage" with no line, never `storefront/homepage:0` (gc-3y89). Its card and copied steps are "What to check" (installed apps, app embeds, rescan, Shopify Support, staff access), never "search the theme", and it has no code editor link. Coverage is by source: the scan records `MALICIOUS_SCRIPT:homepage` in `Scan.liveFindingTypes` only when it actually matched a readable homepage, and the differ treats homepage findings as un-audited on any scan without it (flag off, unreachable or password storefront, unpublished theme, legacy scan), so they are never new or fixed across such a scan, in the app, the diff page or the summary email. While the check runs (both flags on), Home's Theme health "Known malicious code" row is "Clean" only when the scan also read the homepage; otherwise it reads "None found" with the reason (homepage unreadable, not run on that scan, or unpublished theme), never "Clean" (gc-3y89). A step retry always clears the earlier attempt's homepage rows, whatever the flag says at retry time (gc-3y89). Theme-file `MALICIOUS_SCRIPT` is unaffected. (2) **The store speed test**: every URL in Lighthouse's `network-requests` for each run is matched, and the listed domains requested in ANY ok run are stored on that page of the `SpeedReport` (`pages[].maliciousDomains`, no new column or table). They are shown only on the Store speed page (Home has no speed dependency, owner decision 2026-10-11), as one Security risk banner per domain naming the tested pages ("Seen in your {date} speed test"), on EVERY plan, from the shop's latest report with results of any origin, so a downgraded shop still sees its last paid report's hit (only the domain, page and date; none of the report). On a plan without the recurring report (the one free test, or a downgraded shop) no newer test replaces it, so the banner reads "Found in the speed test on {date}" and says it may no longer be current; it is never hidden (gc-3y89). Paid plans read the speed reports once per page load. The Sidekick `get_speed_summary` tool does not carry them yet (gc-3y89 item 9, deferred to release 2 slice 6, which owns the extension's instructions). Speed hits never enter a scan, the differ or an email. Off = no matching, nothing stored, loader data unchanged. **Rollback:** turning the flag off is always safe (nothing new is matched or stored; stored homepage findings stay shown, honestly labelled). Before rolling the CODE back to a version without this check, delete the stored homepage rows first, or the old code shows them as theme references with a broken code editor link and its differ reports them "fixed" on the next scan and in the summary email: `DELETE FROM "Finding" WHERE "findingType" = 'MALICIOUS_SCRIPT' AND filename = 'storefront/homepage';` (speed-test hits need nothing: old code ignores `maliciousDomains`). Known gap: a checkout hijack that loads its payload from an app's API response and only contacts the phishing host on a checkout click (the 2026-10 Hulk/Swym/Selleasy pattern) is caught by neither source.

App no longer active (gc-frda, decision 4A): Home's banner naming apps the latest scan found no longer active in the store (and how many items each left) shows in full on every plan, Free included; its links open that app's view of the scan (`?app=`). On that view Free gets the same context banner as paid, and its summary card, preview rows and upgrade teaser are scoped to that app's non-ignored findings (same formula: up to 5 in full, never more than half), so the items themselves stay behind the Free preview gate. Malicious findings stay in the security alert on every plan and are never previewed or counted as locked. `?app=` for an app with no such record leaves Free's scan-wide view unchanged. Still one upgrade ask per page.

Return-visit banner (gc-97k.9, Free only): on a later results visit (first successful scan completed 24h+ ago), a banner at the top of the results ("Ready to clean up the rest?") repeats the teaser's body and CTA (same copy source) with a "Not now". While it shows, the inline teaser is hidden, so the page has one upgrade ask, and the teaser's `shown` is not recorded on that view. It is pending only while the shop's LATEST successful scan hides findings (2+ non-malicious findings under the up-to-5 formula), so a shop whose results never hide anything never has it pending. It is an interruptive prompt under the cross-prompt cap (`app/lib/prompt-cap.ts`): strict global priority (native review popup > return banner > home feedback nudge), computed per shop whatever page is loading, so while the banner is the shop's pending prompt Home shows no prompt, and at most one distinct prompt per 24h. A higher prompt blocks lower ones for at most 7 days after it became eligible; after that a lower prompt may show on its own page, and the higher one still shows on its page when the slot is free. It re-shows at most weekly, persists on reloads within its 24h window, and retires after three "Not now" clicks. Its shown / clicked / dismissed / converted events are recorded once per merchant; a Free -> paid upgrade converts each ask (teaser, banner) the merchant was shown.

Stale-results banner (gc-mgi, Free and Standard): when a theme was published after the scan being viewed completed (the same condition as Home's theme-change nudge, `app/lib/stale-results.ts`), the top of that scan's results says "These results are from {date}. Your theme changed on {date}, so some findings may already be fixed." Professional sees nothing (auto-rescan on publish, as on Home). On the shop's latest successful scan the banner adds one action: "Rescan now" (Home's scan-start action) when the quota allows a scan; "A new scan is already running." when one is; on Free with the monthly quota used, "Your next free scan is available {date}." plus the trial-vs-upgrade CTA (same copy source as the teaser, `src=stale_results`); on Standard with the weekly quota used, "Your next scan is available {date}." plus Home's "Upgrade for more scans" Settings link (the link only when a higher plan exists, never on Professional, gc-d0ui). The next date is the start of the next quota period (1st of next month 00:00 UTC for Free, next Monday 00:00 UTC for Standard), returned by `canStartScan` as `nextScanAt`. An older scan's page shows the dates and a "View latest results" link instead. The banner is content, not a capped prompt. While it shows the Free CTA it is the page's only upgrade ask: the return banner is not renderable on that view (excluded before the prompt slot is claimed, so its slot is never used up) and the teaser renders without its CTA (and without recording its `shown`). Its shown / clicked / converted events are recorded once per merchant, `shown` only when the CTA renders.

### Store speed report (gc-wt3y, LIVE since 2026-10-11: `SPEED_REPORT_LIVE_ENABLED=true`)

Standard and Professional (`PlanFeatures.speedReport`; Standard since gc-d0ui). Professional is tested weekly and has "Run speed test now"; Standard gets the same report (homepage + product page, per-app cost table) monthly, with no Run now and no weekly cadence (`speedReportCadence`, `speedRunNow`). Free gets the free speed test below instead; a plan with neither (only if `freeSpeedTest` is narrowed) sees a locked teaser on the Store speed page with the Professional upgrade button. Standard's page shows its reports (a FREE_TEST report from before the change included, in the normal history), says "Tested once a month", shows "Next monthly test: {date}" in place of the Run-now button, says "Your first monthly speed test runs on {date}." when no report exists yet, and a failed run says "We'll try again next month." It has no upgrade ask (none existed on the full report view). The Run-now action refuses a Standard shop (`requestSpeedReportRunNow` checks `speedRunNow`; the worker's MANUAL gate too). The page's upgrade ask (teaser, or the free test's ask) records shown / clicked / converted as the `speed_teaser` nudge funnel, once per merchant per stage, converted = any upgrade to Professional by a merchant who saw it (gc-iirv). Google PageSpeed Insights, mobile, performance only, for the homepage and one product page (oldest active product with an online store URL via the optional `read_products` scope; without it, gc-9mbw picks the oldest product from the public `https://<primary host>/products.json?limit=250` through the storefront fetcher's guarded GET, read only after the zero-PageSpeed-cost password pre-check found no password page, and only when that is unavailable (password page, 404, non-JSON, empty) is the report homepage only). 3 runs per page, the median by score is reported (each call may take up to 240 s, `PAGESPEED_TIMEOUT_MS`; a call that times out ends that page's remaining runs, keeping any earlier good run, gc-zees), with the cost broken down by app via the scanner's app matcher. Weekly from its own cron (`speed-report-weekly`, Sunday 08:10 UTC) plus a merchant "Run speed test now" (Professional) limited to once per 24h per shop. **Monthly for Standard (gc-d0ui):** the same cron also selects Standard shops on the first Sunday of each month (a Sunday on day 1 to 7, UTC; `speedReportPlansDue` reads each plan's cadence from the plan matrix). One cron rather than a second monthly one: Standard's runs reuse the coordinator, its per-day event idempotency key, the worker's concurrency 1, the daily budget, the plan/flag/install re-checks and the existing heartbeat unchanged (no new function, no new heartbeat expectation). Cost: about 6 PageSpeed calls per Standard shop per month against the 5,000/day budget; SCHEDULED runs count as paid, so the free-test ceiling still keeps paying shops first. Trade-off: the first-Sunday batch runs Professional + Standard single file under the worker's 6 h start timeout (about 120 shops at ~3 min each), so revisit concurrency before Standard grows past that. Guardrails: global daily call budget `PAGESPEED_DAILY_CALL_BUDGET` (default 5000, counted before each call), each call its own Inngest step, concurrency 1, plan/flag/install re-checked before every call. Key: `PAGESPEED_API_KEY`. Password-protected storefronts get an UNAVAILABLE_PASSWORD report. Known limit: some apps skip loading under Lighthouse (gc-xewx), so their cost is under-reported; the page says so.

**Free speed test (Free only since gc-d0ui, `PlanFeatures.freeSpeedTest`).** Every plan without the report gets ONE homepage-only test, once ever per shop. Standard had it until 2026-10-19; it now has the monthly report instead (`freeSpeedTest: false`), and a Standard shop that used its free test still sees that report in its history. The Store speed page shows "Run your free speed test" while it is unused. The run is the same `speed-report-run` worker with origin `FREE_TEST`: homepage only (no Admin product query, no `/products.json`, a product-page call is refused even if asked for), same 3 runs + median, password pre-check, concurrency and budget reservation (against the lower free ceiling below; no queue priority), and every gate re-checks the plan and that the test is still unused. The cron never tests Free. Claims are atomic: the action claims the shared 24h slot (`Shop.speedReportRequestedAt`) AND `freeSpeedTestUsedAt IS NULL` in one conditional write, and refuses while any run is in flight. The test is used up (`Shop.freeSpeedTestUsedAt`, written in the same transaction as the report) ONLY by a report that COMPLETED with a homepage score; a password page, failure, budget skip, no score, dispatch failure or cancel leaves it unused (the merchant may try again 24h after the last try; a dead or cancelled run hands the slot back like Run now). Attempt cap: a shop gets at most `FREE_SPEED_TEST_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (5) attempts in total, and only an attempt that actually reached Google counts: a FREE_TEST report with `psiCalls > 0` that was not stopped by the daily ceiling (no new column). `psiCalls` is counted on the report call by call, before each call is sent (gc-au2l), so a run whose worker died or got stuck after calling Google counts too, even though its 24h slot is handed back. A run that never called Google never burns an attempt (dispatch failure, a worker that died or got stuck before its first call, a missing key, an ineligible run), and neither does a password page, which our own pre-check finds before any call. Once 5 counted attempts exist and none used the test up, the page says the free test couldn't complete (Google's test timed out or couldn't load the homepage), shows the support address and the page's one upgrade ask, and the action refuses before any claim. The 5th attempt still uses the test up if it COMPLETES; the cap never gates the worker. Paying shops come first: FREE_TEST calls reserve against `FREE_SPEED_TEST_DAILY_CALL_CEILING` (600) on the SAME daily counter, so free tests stop once the day's total reaches 600 and paid reports keep the rest of `PAGESPEED_DAILY_CALL_BUDGET` (a budget set below the default 5000 lowers the free ceiling in proportion, `freeSpeedTestDailyCallCeiling`: 500 gives 60); a free test stopped by it shows "Free speed tests are paused for today", is not used up and is not counted. FREE_TEST gets no queue priority (only a Professional Run now does). Result for Free: the homepage score and core timings, plus one line naming the costliest app by processing time with its download size (the summary email's sentence, `costliestAppLine`), dated and read-only once used, then ONE upgrade ask to Professional (weekly tests, a product page, every app's cost). The loader sends only that: never the per-app table, other third parties, first-party totals, the product page, other reports or a re-run. A shop that upgrades to Professional sees its free report in the normal history; a shop that downgrades from Professional sees only FREE_TEST reports and may use the free test once if it never did. Telemetry: free tests are `speed_report` OpsEvents with origin `FREE_TEST` and appear in the operator digest's SPEED REPORTS section ("free test N" in Runs, plus "Free tests: started N, used up M"); upgrade clicks are the `speed_teaser` funnel.

### Theme health (gc-i6du)

Positioning: "theme health" / "theme checker" searches have no purpose-built incumbent, and the broad scanners lead with a 0-100 score. GhostCode answers the same question with what old apps left behind and what it costs, as a plain list. **No score, grade, percentage or "x of y" headline** (the 0-100 score was removed on 2026-10-09, gc-k2ub), and it stays read-only.

**Checklist (Home, every plan).** Under the lanes, Home lists what the latest successful scan checked, one row per category, each "Clean" or "N issues" with a concrete cost from counts (extra requests, Liquid errors on the homepage, leftover records). Every `FindingType` belongs to exactly one category (`HEALTH_CATEGORY_OF` in `app/lib/theme-health.ts`, an exhaustive Record): Known malicious code; Liquid errors on your homepage (its own row, so a storefront check is never masked by theme-file checks); Broken links and leftover app text; Leftover scripts and styles; Leftover snippets and sections; SEO and AI tags; Tracking still running; Store data left behind; Script tag deadline. Two more rows are not findings: **Theme type and version** and **Unpublished themes**.

- **Never "Clean" about something nobody checked.** A missing permission, an unreadable storefront, a size cap, a plan-gated check or a scan of an unpublished theme reads "None found" (part of the row was not checked) or "Not checked", with the reason. So does a check that is switched on NOW but that the scan being shown did not run (a scan from before the check shipped, a scan on which the check failed, or a legacy scan that recorded nothing about what it ran): "Not checked on your last scan. Run a new scan to try again." Only a check that is off for everyone right now (a soft-launch flag) is left out of its row, and a row with nothing switched on is not shown. The checklist never throws: a failure in it is logged and Home renders without the section.
- **Gating: none needed, by construction.** A row carries counts and fixed copy only (never a file, line, snippet or app name), so Free gets the same rows as paid plans. "View findings" opens the scan page, where each plan's own gating applies: the type-filtered list on Standard and Professional when the row holds one type, the scan page itself otherwise and always on Free. The checklist adds no upgrade ask: on Free the two plan-gated checks are named in plain text ("Broken links are checked on Standard and Professional.", "Old checkout code is checked on Standard and Professional.") with no button or link, so Home keeps its one-ask rule.
- **Cost per Home load:** only with results on screen (never on the 3s poll while a scan runs): one small read of the unpublished themes' latest scans. Free now reads the theme list (same 60s cache as the picker) for this section only; the picker and the multi-theme nudge are unchanged.
- **Not shown: KB and processing time from the speed report.** Home must render with no dependency on speed data (`tests/integration/speed-report-decoupling.test.ts`, owner decision 2026-10-11), so those numbers stay on the Store speed page. Adding them here needs an owner decision to allow a second read-only bridge.

**Liquid errors on your homepage (`LIQUID_ERROR_RENDERED`, all plans).** The published theme's public homepage, already read once per scan for script tags, is also read for ONE kind of Liquid error: a missing snippet ("Liquid error (layout/theme line 309): Could not find asset snippets/x.liquid", the classic leftover of an app that deleted its snippet). No extra request. Page text alone is never trusted (a blog excerpt, a review or a tutorial can print the same words): a finding is emitted only when the scanned theme confirms it, meaning `snippets/x.liquid` does not exist in the theme AND a theme file renders it (`render` / `include`), and when the error names a file, that file is one that renders it. Everything else is dropped silently: other Liquid errors (syntax errors, "divided by 0", app-block errors) and anything the theme does not confirm. Missing a real error is accepted; a false one is not. One finding per (file, snippet), at most 10, HIGH, lane "Customers see it"; a snippet that matches an app signature names that app. The copy says the homepage "prints" the error and that it can be inside a menu or a hidden section; it does not claim shoppers see it. Same gates as the request: `SCRIPT_TAG_SUNSET_LIVE_ENABLED`, published theme only, and a password page or unreadable storefront records the category in `Scan.unreachableCategories` (never "fixed", and never "new" against a scan that could not check it). A failure inside this check is isolated: it is logged, yields no Liquid finding, marks the check as not run for that scan, and never affects the script-tag audit or the scan. Not plan-gated and not always-visible: it follows the normal Free preview (up to 5 in full, never more than half). An app-level ignore does not hide it (an instance ignore does). **Stored per finding:** only text we rebuild in a fixed shape from three parsed parts, each limited to letters, digits and `. _ / -`: the theme file path, the line number and the snippet name (plus the app name from our own signature list). No page HTML and no other page text is stored or logged.

**Theme type and version (all plans).** Each scan records `Scan.themeInfo` from files it already fetched: the theme's name, version and author (`config/settings_schema.json` `theme_info`) and whether it is Online Store 2.0 (has a JSON template) or **vintage** (Liquid templates only). A vintage theme is flagged on the checklist; it is not a finding, so it never changes finding counts, the Free preview, the diff or the summary email. "Version behind latest" is not shown: there is no public source for a theme's latest version.

**Unpublished themes (all plans).** The checklist counts the themes shoppers do not see against Shopify's 20-theme limit (development themes excluded), and for any that have a scan shows that scan's finding count. Scanning a theme other than the published one is still Professional-only (`maxThemes`); a count shown here comes from a scan the shop already ran.

### Shopify Sidekick tools (gc-5nok, built, NOT deployed)

**Sidekick works on every plan; each tool gates exactly as the app page it reads.** Two read-only tools (`extensions/sidekick-tools`, backends `/api/sidekick/latest-scan` and `/api/sidekick/speed`), no extra price and no plan of their own:

- `get_latest_scan_summary`: Home's view of the latest successful scan on every plan (the findings tile's total, ignored malicious code added back; counts by impact area; Theme health verdict). Its top findings are Home's "Start here" rows, so Free gets only its preview rows plus the never-paywalled findings; each row's `impact` is its lane, "Security risk" for known malicious code, or "Security advisory" for an affected-app advisory (the label the extension's instructions key their guardrail on). Known malicious code is listed in full on EVERY plan, ignored findings included, as the scan page's security alert lists it. A Theme health reason that names a plan (Free's plan-gated checks) is returned as "Not included in the current plan." so a Sidekick answer never reads as a plan pitch.
- `get_speed_summary`: the Store speed page's own plan-gated read (`readSpeedPageReport`). Standard and Professional get the latest report with results (each tested page, top 5 apps by processing time); `scope` says "The latest monthly speed report with results: ..." on Standard and "The latest speed report with results: ..." on Professional, and with no report yet Standard is told the date of its first monthly report (gc-d0ui). Free gets only its one free homepage test (score, timings, the ONE costliest app). A plan with neither gets `not_available_on_plan`. While `SPEED_REPORT_LIVE_ENABLED` is off the tool says the report is not available.
- No upgrade wording anywhere in the extension or its responses (App Store rules 2.2.8 / 2.2.9). `not_available_on_plan` is a plain statement, and the instructions tell Sidekick never to suggest changing plans.

### Merchant summary emails (gc-ol95, LIVE since 2026-10-09)

One SUMMARY email per store (multipart: plain text plus an equivalent HTML version) after each SCHEDULED scan: Professional weekly, Standard monthly, Free never (`PlanFeatures.alertCadence` in `app/lib/billing.server.ts`: `none` / `monthly` / `weekly`, matching each plan's scheduled-scan cadence, gc-iefo). Manual and auto-publish scans never send. It replaced the per-change "new findings" alert (gc-syz.5): merchants test many apps, so per-change mail would be spam. `canReceiveAlerts(plan)` and `getAlertWindowMs(plan)` (`app/lib/plan-gating.server.ts`) are the gate and the throttle window (weekly 7 days, monthly 28 days; at most one summary per window, throttled only inside 90% of it so scheduler jitter never blocks the next one). Any plan name that is not Standard or Professional resolves to Free.

**Nothing changed = no email.** "Changed" since the last summary sent to the shop = new findings, fixed findings, an app newly no longer active (AppRemoval REMOVED on a scan since then) or an app's leftovers newly cleaned up (CLEANED since then); an app that went inactive and came back (REINSTALLED) is omitted. The finding diff uses the in-app diff's guards (ignores, `scanDiffOptions` coverage, `restrictToLiveInBoth`), so a permission grant or a newly live detector is never "new". Baseline = the scan the last summary covered (ledger), else the theme's previous scan. The same information is never sent twice.

**Store speed section (gc-2wnf, Professional; Standard since gc-d0ui; LIVE since 2026-10-11 with `SPEED_REPORT_LIVE_ENABLED=true`).** The summary adds two lines and a Store speed link ("Last speed test (Oct 11, 2026): your homepage scored 62 / 100, up 5 from the test before." / "Klaviyo used 4.0 s of processing time and 621 KB on your homepage.") only when the plan has the speed report (Standard or Professional), a COMPLETED speed report exists inside the plan's window (8 days weekly; 35 days monthly, because Standard's email on the 1st carries the report from the previous month's first Sunday, 21 to 31 days old), it is newer than the last summary sent (one report is never emailed twice), and it is the first report with a homepage result or changed materially vs the previous one (homepage score moved 5+ points on the same URL, the costliest app changed, or an app entered the top 3 by processing time). A material speed change alone is a reason to send. The email follows the 06:40 scan and the speed cron runs at 08:10, so it usually carries last week's report (`app/services/summary-speed.server.ts`).

**Named speed regressions (gc-vgqc, Professional only).** On the weekly cadence the speed section also NAMES up to 3 homepage apps that are new or got costlier vs the previous comparable report (the newest earlier report with a homepage result), each with its delta, followed by the "Some apps skip loading during speed tests, so their real cost can be higher." caveat. An app is named when it is NEW (in the current per-app list, absent from the previous one) with a non-trivial cost, or when its download size rose by at least 100 KB (`SPEED_REGRESSION_MIN_BYTES`) or its processing time by at least 200 ms (`SPEED_REGRESSION_MIN_MS`) AND that rise is at least 25% of its previous value (`SPEED_REGRESSION_MIN_RATIO`); a new app is measured from zero against the same floors. Noise guards: both homepage results must be OK and have tested the same URL, otherwise nothing is named (so an app is never "new" because the previous page failed or tested another URL); only matched apps, never unmatched third-party hosts; no naming on a shop's first report. Order: biggest processing-time rise, then biggest download-size rise, then name (`SPEED_REGRESSION_MAX_APPS` = 3). A named app is itself a material change, so it alone sends the email. Copy: "Klaviyo is new since the test before and adds 140 KB and 0.3 s of processing time on your homepage." / "Judge.me's processing time on your homepage rose from 0.4 s to 0.9 s." (or "download size ... rose from 120 KB to 340 KB", or both in one sentence); "processing time", never "script time", and never checkout timing. The Store speed page (Professional) badges the same apps in each tested page's per-app table, latest report vs the one before it: "New", "Up 0.5 s", "Up 120 KB" (`speedAppRegressions` in `app/lib/speed-report.ts`, the one rule for both). Standard's monthly email and page keep the section and table without naming (gc-d0ui). No new email type: the one-week lag of the summary email is accepted.

**Consent: nobody gets an email without knowing it is on.** Sendable only if the plan is Standard/Professional AND `Shop.alertsEnabled` AND (`summaryNoticeShownAt` OR `summaryOptedInAt`) AND not uninstalled AND an owner email is known AND the sender is configured. A Free -> paid move (trial included) after this shipped sets `summaryNoticePendingAt` (billing reconciler), unless the shop declined the gc-xorg opt-in below; the next paid Home load (once sending is configured) shows "Summary emails are on" once and stamps `summaryNoticeShownAt`. Turning the Settings toggle on while sending is live records `summaryOptedInAt` (consent). Turning it on while sending is dark is NOT consent on its own (the card promised "We'll let you know before any are sent"): it only turns the toggle on and owes the Home notice, so that merchant becomes eligible once the notice is shown (Nathan Q9=9A). Shops paid before this shipped (e.g. paw-naturals) get no notice and no email unless they opt in; no consent field is backfilled. Those shops are asked once on Home (gc-xorg): "New: summary emails" with Turn on / No thanks, an interruptive prompt under the cross-prompt cap (`summary_optin`, after the return banner and before the feedback nudge; Home only). Eligible only when the plan has summaries, sending is configured, `alertsEnabled` is on (never nag a merchant who turned it off or unsubscribed), the shop is installed, and it holds no consent stamp, no owed notice and no `summaryOptInDeclinedAt`. Turn on is the Settings opt-in (`summaryOptedInAt`), accepted only while the shop is still fully eligible (a stale tab never undoes an unsubscribe or a No thanks; Settings stays the way to re-enable); No thanks stamps `summaryOptInDeclinedAt` once and changes nothing else, and a later Free -> paid move does not owe the notice. Ignored, it may return on a later visit after the cap's 24h window, but it stops being offered 14 days after it was first shown (gc-rxq0: the first render stamps `summaryOptInFirstShownAt` once; at 14 days it is no longer eligible, `SUMMARY_OPTIN_OFFER_MS`, so it cannot starve the feedback nudge below it). Retirement writes nothing: it is not a decline, `alertsEnabled` is untouched and Settings still opts in; a Turn on from a tab older than that is ignored like any other ineligible accept. The Settings checkbox shows "on" only when the toggle is on and the merchant knows or is owed the notice; a shop never told and owing nothing reads "off".

Framing: a digest, never a status alert. Subject is always `GhostCode digest for {Store Name}` (both plans, no app names); the body opens `Here is your weekly digest for {Store Name} from GhostCode.` (`monthly` on Standard). Store Name = `Shop.storeName`, read from Admin `shop { name }` in the same query as the owner email and cached alongside it (falls back to the myshopify domain; control characters stripped, capped at 80 chars). Content: counts and app names only (never code snippets, file names, customer data, upsell, or a "removed"/"uninstalled" claim), ending in a footer with the sender's identity (Alpenglow Software LLC, support@alpenglowsoftware.com; the HTML adds the Alpenglow logo) and an Unsubscribe link. No postal address. Ledger: one `MerchantAlert` row per summary (unique per shop + scan; new/fixed/inactive/cleaned counts). Owner email and store name from Admin `shop { email name }` (no extra scope) cached on `Shop.alertEmail` and `Shop.storeName`. All of it is deleted on shop/redact. Sends only while `MERCHANT_ALERTS_ENABLED=true`, `RESEND_API_KEY` and `MERCHANT_ALERT_FROM` are all set (all set in prod since 2026-10-09); without them Settings says "Summary emails are coming soon".

## Downgrade & Cancellation

- **Professional → Standard**: In-app "Switch to Standard" button on settings page. Uses `replacementBehavior: APPLY_ON_NEXT_BILLING_CYCLE` — merchant keeps Professional features until current billing cycle ends.
- **Paid → Free**: Cancel link to `shopify://admin/settings/billing`. Shopify handles cancellation. Cancellations have NO redirect back to the app, so the plan reverts to free via the on-load reconcile backstop (see Billing Mechanics), not a webhook.
- **Billing events tracked**: All upgrades, downgrades, and cancellations recorded in `BillingEvent` table for analytics.

## Upgrade Triggers

### Free → Standard

1. **Scan limit hit** — First scan is always free. After that, free users get 1/month; hitting that limit surfaces an upgrade prompt. Monthly quota resets on the 1st of each calendar month.
2. **Finding details locked** — Free users see severity counts, category breakdown (Scripts/Styles/Snippets/Sections/Orphan Assets), and up to 5 findings in full: `max(1, min(5, floor(total / 2)))` of the non-malicious, non-ignored findings, picked by the shared "Start here" ranking (severity, then urgency, then lane; gc-bn0x) and one per consequence lane before any lane repeats (gc-97k.10). All remaining findings are locked server-side. An upgrade banner explains what's behind the paywall.

### Standard → Professional

3. **Scan limit hit on Standard** — Standard plan allows 1 manual scan per week (resets Monday 00:00 UTC). Weekly limit reached surfaces an upgrade prompt to Professional (up to 5 scans a day, gc-d0ui). Professional at its daily cap gets no upgrade prompt, only the next-available time. Combined with auto-rescan being Pro-only, this creates meaningful upgrade pressure for active merchants.
4. **Auto-rescan skipped** — Theme publish webhooks arrive but scans are silently skipped on non-Pro plans. Standard shops get a monthly scheduled scan but miss same-day feedback when they publish a theme. Passive trigger (user doesn't see it unless they notice scans aren't auto-running).
5. **Scan diffing unavailable** — Standard users don't see "New / Resolved / Unchanged" diff badges. Pro users get change tracking.
6. **Multi-theme gating** — `canUseMultipleThemes()` (in `app/lib/plan-gating.server.ts`, derived from the plan matrix's `maxThemes`) gates multi-theme scanning to Professional. Standard is capped at 1 theme, so the theme picker is disabled there; a Standard shop whose store has more than one theme also sees an in-app upgrade nudge on the dashboard.

## Billing Mechanics

- All billing goes through **Shopify's native billing API** (merchants pay via their Shopify invoice)
- Plan stored as string on `Shop` model in Prisma (`plan` field, default `"free"`)
- Plan changes are detected on app load — the `APP_SUBSCRIPTIONS_UPDATE` webhook is **DEAD as of 2026-04-28** (Shopify stopped sending it for App Pricing apps)
- Unknown plan names or non-ACTIVE subscription statuses default to `"free"` (safe fallback)
- **Plan reconciliation (source of truth):** with the webhook gone, plan state is driven entirely from the app-load loader (`app/routes/app.tsx`), which always corrects `Shop.plan` from Shopify's `currentAppInstallation.activeSubscriptions` query (`app/services/billing-reconciler.server.ts`) — the URL `plan_handle` param is NEVER trusted to grant a tier. Zero active subscriptions → `free`; if multiple are active, the highest tier wins. Reconciliation never blocks app load (errors are logged and the stored plan stands). It runs on two triggers:
  - **Redirect fast-path:** when a merchant selects/confirms a plan, Shopify redirects back with a `plan_handle` param. Its PRESENCE forces an immediate reconcile (bypassing the freshness window) so upgrades grant right away. Because this correction is merchant-initiated, it records a `BillingEvent` (restoring the analytics the webhook used to produce). If the Admin API call fails on this path, exactly one retry is attempted — a permanent loss is otherwise possible because backstop reconciles run with `recordEvent: false` and will not re-record the missed event.
  - **Backstop:** otherwise, reconcile only when the stored plan is older than a **1-hour** freshness window (`Shop.planReconciledAt`, tunable). This covers out-of-redirect changes (cancellations, freezes, expirations, which have no redirect). Routine backstop corrections are logged but do **not** create `BillingEvent` rows, to avoid polluting conversion/churn analytics.
  - Both triggers stamp `planReconciledAt`, resetting the freshness clock. Plan-name → tier mapping lives in `app/lib/billing.server.ts` (`resolvePlanFromSubscription`).
- Test mode: `SHOPIFY_BILLING_TEST=true` env var; dev store uses test charges automatically

## Pricing Considerations & Future Options

### Current assessment

- Free → Standard gate is strong: hiding details creates real urgency
- Standard → Professional gap is $20 ($9 → $29); Pro's incremental value (up to 5 scans a day, multi-theme, auto-rescan, diffing, weekly speed report with Run now) has to carry it
- 7-day trial on both paid tiers lowers friction
- Pricing is now at/below the direct-comp median (see 2026-09-18 decision-log entry). Deliberately a land-grab price: no pricing power exists pre-reviews, so the goal is install velocity → reviews → social proof, then raise (grandfathering early merchants)

### Options to consider

- **Annual discount** (e.g., ~$90/yr Standard, ~$290/yr Pro — roughly 2 months free) — common in Shopify apps, improves retention
- **Usage-based pricing** — charge per theme on Standard instead of hard-capping at 1
- **Raise prices post-traction** — once reviews accumulate and WTP is observed, revisit; the $9/$29 floor was chosen for the zero-review stage, not as a permanent ceiling

### Open questions

- Monitor post-launch willingness-to-pay on Pro ($29/mo). If conversion is strong and churn is low, this is the first lever to raise. Watch for agency/developer buyers who may tolerate a higher price point.
- **Agency tier (post-launch):** Is there demand for an agency-focused tier above Pro? Watch for agencies adopting Pro at scale (multiple stores per billing email, export/reporting feature requests). An agency tier would require a multi-store dashboard (significant architecture change — separate web surface outside Shopify admin), white-label reporting, and flat per-agency pricing. Low-lift precursor: add exportable PDF reports to Pro and watch uptake. Do not design for this pre-launch.
- Should auto-rescan be surfaced more actively (e.g., "a theme was published but auto-rescan is a Pro feature")? Research shows behavioral triggers convert 3–4x better than generic nudges — strong post-launch candidate.
- Is multi-theme gating worth enforcing in the UI before launch, or defer to post-launch data?
